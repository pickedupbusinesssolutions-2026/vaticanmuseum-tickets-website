Christmas Mass at the Vatican 2026

There are two main Christmas events at the Vatican. The Christmas Eve "Midnight Mass" is held on 24 December at 10:00 pm inside St. Peter's Basilica, led by Pope Leo XIV, and you need a free ticket from the Prefecture of the Papal Household to attend. On Christmas Day, 25 December, the Pope gives the Urbi et Orbi blessing from the balcony of St. Peter's Basilica at noon, out in St. Peter's Square, and that needs no ticket. Both draw huge crowds, so arrive hours early, and you can watch either live online if you cannot be there. The Vatican confirms exact times each December, so check closer to the date.

Christmas Mass at the Vatican is one of the most moving things you can do in Rome over the holiday, and also one of the busiest.

Most people are really asking about two separate events: the Mass on Christmas Eve, which is inside the Basilica and needs a ticket, and the Christmas Day blessing, which is outdoors in the square and open to all.

This guide explains both, with the times, how to attend, how to get the free tickets for the Mass, how crowded it gets, and where to watch if you cannot make it in person.

Christmas Eve Midnight Mass

The main Christmas Mass is the Mass of the Night, still called Midnight Mass by tradition, held on 24 December 2026. In recent years the Pope has celebrated it at 10:00 pm, not literally at midnight, inside St. Peter's Basilica, and Pope Leo XIV leads it.

To attend inside the Basilica you need a free ticket (more on that below), and the doors open a few hours before the Mass. It is a full liturgy of about two hours, in Latin and Italian with some other languages, and seating is limited, so a ticket does not guarantee a close view. If you do not have a ticket, you can still watch it on the big screens set up in St. Peter's Square.

Christmas Day: The Urbi et Orbi Blessing

On Christmas Day, 25 December 2026, the Pope will give the Urbi et Orbi blessing, which means "to the city and to the world," from the central balcony of St. Peter's Basilica at 12:00 noon. This is the famous moment you see on the news, with the square packed below.

The blessing is out in St. Peter's Square and is open to everyone with no ticket needed, so you simply turn up. It is short, around half an hour with the Pope's Christmas message and the blessing, but the square fills early. There is also a papal Mass on Christmas morning before the blessing, which does need a ticket like the Midnight Mass.

How to Get Tickets for Christmas Mass

Tickets for the papal Masses inside the Basilica, including the Christmas Eve Midnight Mass, are free. They are not sold, and they are handed out by the Prefecture of the Papal Household, the Vatican office that manages papal ceremonies. A few things to know:

  • Request them well in advance - You ask the Prefecture of the Papal Household in writing, and demand far outstrips supply at Christmas, so request as early as you can.
  • Collection - If granted, tickets are usually collected in person near the Bronze Doors, just off St. Peter's Square, in the days before the Mass.
  • They are free - Anyone asking you to pay for a Christmas Mass ticket is not official, so avoid them.
  • No ticket for the blessing - The Christmas Day Urbi et Orbi blessing in the square needs no ticket at all.

Because the process and the request details can change year to year, confirm the current steps on the Vatican's official channels before you plan around it.

How Crowded Is It and When to Arrive

Very crowded, to be blunt. Christmas is one of the busiest times of the year at the Vatican, and both the Midnight Mass and the Christmas Day blessing draw tens of thousands of people.

For the Midnight Mass, even with a ticket, arrive two to three hours early to clear security and find a seat, as the queues are long and the Basilica fills.

For the Urbi et Orbi blessing on Christmas Day, get to St. Peter's Square well before noon, ideally a couple of hours ahead, if you want a decent spot.

Dress warmly, as December in Rome is cold and you will be standing outside for a long time, and go through security with as little as possible.

Where to Watch Christmas Mass Online

If you cannot be there, or you would rather stay warm, both the Midnight Mass and the Urbi et Orbi blessing are broadcast live around the world.

Vatican Media streams them on the official Vatican YouTube channel and website, and many national broadcasters carry them too. The live stream is often the better view of the Mass itself, since the cameras are closer than most of the crowd ever gets.

Other Christmas and New Year Services

The Pope leads several other services over the holiday period, most inside St. Peter's Basilica:

  • 26 December (St. Stephen): the Angelus at noon in St. Peter's Square, no ticket needed.
  • 31 December: First Vespers and the Te Deum at 5:00 PM, a service of thanksgiving for the year, with limited tickets.
  • 1 January (World Day of Peace): a papal Mass at 10:00 AM, then the Angelus at noon in the square.
  • 6 January (Epiphany): a papal Mass in the morning.

The Vatican Museums themselves are closed on 25 and 26 December, so if you want to see the museums and the Sistine Chapel during a Christmas trip, plan them for another day and book a skip-the-line entry ticket in advance. Our opening hours guide lists the full holiday closures.

Frequently Asked Questions

What time is Christmas Mass at the Vatican?

The Christmas Eve Midnight Mass is held on 24 December at 10:00 pm in St. Peter's Basilica. On Christmas Day, 25 December, there is a morning Mass and then the Urbi et Orbi blessing at noon. The Vatican confirms exact times each December.

How do I attend Christmas Mass at the Vatican?

For the Midnight Mass inside the Basilica you need a free ticket from the Prefecture of the Papal Household, requested well in advance. The Christmas Day Urbi et Orbi blessing in St. Peter's Square needs no ticket, so you can just turn up.

Are Christmas Mass tickets free?

Yes. Tickets for the papal Masses are free and handed out by the Prefecture of the Papal Household, never sold. Anyone charging you for a Christmas Mass ticket is not official.

Is Christmas Day at the Vatican crowded?

Yes, very. Both the Midnight Mass and the Christmas Day blessing draw tens of thousands of people. Arrive two to three hours early for the Mass, and well before noon for the Urbi et Orbi blessing in the square.

Where can I watch Christmas Mass at the Vatican?

Live on Vatican Media's official YouTube channel and website, and on many national TV broadcasters. The Midnight Mass and the Urbi et Orbi blessing are both streamed worldwide, often with a better view than the crowd gets.

Can I visit the Vatican Museums on Christmas Day?

No. The Vatican Museums and the Sistine Chapel are closed on 25 and 26 December. Plan your museum visit for another day during your trip and book a ticket ahead.

What time is Christmas Eve Mass at the Vatican?

It is at 10:00 pm on 24 December in St. Peter's Basilica, despite the "Midnight Mass" name. Doors open a few hours before, and you need a free ticket to go inside.
