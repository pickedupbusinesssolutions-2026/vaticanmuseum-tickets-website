St. Peter's Basilica

St. Peter's Basilica is the great church in Vatican City, one of the largest in the world, built over the tomb of St. Peter the Apostle. The current building went up between 1506 and 1626, with work by Bramante, Michelangelo, Maderno, and Bernini. It is free to enter and sits on St. Peter's Square, with its own entrance separate from the Vatican Museums. The highlights are Michelangelo's Pietà, Bernini's bronze baldachin, and the dome, which you can climb for a fee. The dress code of covered shoulders and knees is enforced.

St. Peter's Basilica is the one part of the Vatican that costs nothing to enter and that almost everyone can see, yet many visitors are unsure how it relates to the museums next door or what they are actually looking at inside.

It is not part of the Vatican Museums route, it is a separate church, and it holds some of the most important art and architecture in the Christian world.

This guide covers what the basilica is, where it stands, its history, what to see inside, climbing the dome, how to visit, and how to attend Mass.

What Is St. Peter's Basilica?

St. Peter's Basilica is the most important church of the Catholic Church and one of the largest churches anywhere, able to hold tens of thousands of people.

It stands on the spot where tradition says St. Peter, the first pope, was buried after being martyred in Rome in the first century.

It is not a cathedral, and it is not the pope's own cathedral either, but it is where the pope leads the biggest ceremonies of the Church's year. For visitors, it is a vast, richly decorated space that is free to walk into and a highlight of any trip to Rome.

Where is St. Peter's Basilica?

St. Peter's Basilica is in Vatican City, at the far end of St. Peter's Square, within Rome. The entrance is reached across the square, which is a different place from the Vatican Museums entrance on Viale Vaticano, about a 10 to 15 minute walk around the city walls.

The nearest metro is Ottaviano-San Pietro on Line A. You do not pass any border control to enter, and the square itself is open to everyone. Our St. Peter's Square guide covers the great oval out front.

A Short History

There have been two St. Peter's Basilicas on this site. The first, known as Old St. Peter's, was built by the Emperor Constantine in the fourth century, around 320 AD, over what was believed to be St. Peter's grave. It stood for more than 1,100 years and was the heart of Christian Rome.

By the early 1500s it was crumbling, and Pope Julius II took the bold decision to pull it down and start again. The foundation stone of the new basilica was laid in 1506, and the building took 120 years, finishing in 1626.

A string of great architects shaped it: Bramante began it, Michelangelo designed the dome, Carlo Maderno added the long nave and the façade, and Gian Lorenzo Bernini created much of the interior and the square outside.

What to See Inside

The basilica is enormous, but a few things are not to be missed:

  • Michelangelo's Pietà - Carved when he was only in his twenties, this marble sculpture of Mary cradling the dead Christ is one of the most beautiful works ever made. It sits just inside the entrance, behind glass, on the right.
  • Bernini's baldachin - The towering bronze canopy over the main altar, nearly 30 metres tall, marking the spot above St. Peter's tomb.
  • The dome from inside - Standing under Michelangelo's dome and looking up at the gold lettering around its base is one of the great sights in Rome.
  • The statue of St. Peter - A bronze figure whose right foot has been worn smooth by centuries of pilgrims touching it.
  • St. Peter's tomb - Below the main altar, in the area called the Confessio, you can look down toward the shrine over the Apostle's grave.

Climbing the Dome

Michelangelo's dome, the cupola, can be climbed for one of the best views in Rome, looking straight down St. Peter's Square and across the city. It costs a few euros, separate from the free basilica entry, and you can take a lift partway or walk the whole way.

Either way there is a final stretch of narrow, curving steps to the top, so it is not for anyone who struggles with tight spaces or a long climb. Our St. Peter's Basilica tickets guide covers the dome climb prices, the steps, and how to skip the queue.

Visiting: Hours, Entry, and Dress Code

Entry to St. Peter's Basilica is free. You join a security line in St. Peter's Square, which can be long in peak season, and pass through a screening before going in.

The basilica is usually open from around 7:00 am to 7:00 pm from April to September, and to about 6:30 pm from October to March, with the dome opening a little later.

Two things to plan around. The dress code is strict, with shoulders and knees covered for everyone, as explained in our dress code guide.

And the basilica is often closed or restricted on Wednesday mornings for the Papal Audience, and during major ceremonies, so it is worth checking before you go. Our opening hours guide has the current times.

How to Attend Mass

St. Peter's Basilica holds several Masses a day, and anyone is welcome to attend for free. Masses are held at the various altars throughout the day, with the main celebrations at the papal altar and the Altar of the Chair at the far end.

If you want to attend rather than sightsee, come for one of the scheduled Mass times, dress appropriately, and enter through the normal security line. Large papal Masses on major feast days need free tickets from the Prefecture of the Papal Household and draw big crowds.

St. Peter's Basilica and the Vatican Museums

This is the point that confuses most people. St. Peter's Basilica and the Vatican Museums are two separate visits, with two separate entrances. The museums are a paid, ticketed route that ends in the Sistine Chapel, while the basilica is a free church you enter from the square.

There is one link worth knowing. On a guided tour of the museums, you can pass from the Sistine Chapel straight into St. Peter's Basilica through an internal shortcut, skipping the long security line in the square. On your own you cannot use that door, so you exit the museums and walk around.

The best plan for most people is to book a skip-the-line Vatican Museums ticket, see the museums and Sistine Chapel, then visit the free basilica next door, or take a guided tour that links the two.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is St. Peter's Basilica?

St. Peter's Basilica is the great church in Vatican City, one of the largest in the world, built over the tomb of St. Peter the Apostle. It is the main church used by the pope for major ceremonies and is free to enter.

Where is St. Peter's Basilica?

In Vatican City, at the end of St. Peter's Square, within Rome. The entrance is across the square, separate from the Vatican Museums entrance on Viale Vaticano.

When was St. Peter's Basilica built?

The current basilica was built between 1506 and 1626. It replaced Old St. Peter's, the original church built by the Emperor Constantine in the fourth century.

Is St. Peter's Basilica free to enter?

Yes. Entry to the basilica is free, though you pass through a security line first. Climbing the dome costs a few euros extra.

Do I need a ticket for St. Peter's Basilica?

Not for the basilica itself, which is free. You only pay for extras such as the dome climb or a guided tour. Our St. Peter's Basilica tickets guide covers those.

What is the dress code for St. Peter's Basilica?

Shoulders and knees must be covered for both men and women. This is enforced at the entrance, and you may be turned away if you are not covered up.

Can I attend Mass at St. Peter's Basilica?

Yes. Several Masses are held each day and are free to attend. Large papal Masses on feast days need free tickets from the Prefecture of the Papal Household.
