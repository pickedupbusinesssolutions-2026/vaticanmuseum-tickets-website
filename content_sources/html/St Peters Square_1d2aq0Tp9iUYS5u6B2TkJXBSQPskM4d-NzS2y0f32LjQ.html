What is St. Peter's Square?

St. Peter's Square, or Piazza San Pietro in Italian, is the enormous public plaza in front of St. Peter's Basilica. It is not a square in the usual sense but a great oval, ringed by curved colonnades that reach out like two arms toward the people arriving.

That was the whole idea. Bernini described the colonnades as the motherly arms of the Church, reaching out to embrace visitors, believers and pilgrims alike. The square can hold several hundred thousand people, and it is the stage for the biggest gatherings of the Catholic Church.

Where is St. Peter's Square?

St. Peter's Square is in Vatican City, directly in front of the basilica, within Rome. It is open to all, and you simply walk into it, as there is no border check and no ticket to enter the square itself. Security screening only comes when you go on to enter the basilica.

The nearest metro is Ottaviano-San Pietro on Line A, a short walk away, and the grand avenue of Via della Conciliazione leads to the square from the direction of the river and Castel Sant'Angelo. Our how to get to the Vatican guide covers the routes.

Who Designed It and When

The square was designed by Gian Lorenzo Bernini, the greatest sculptor and architect of Baroque Rome, and built between 1656 and 1667 under Pope Alexander VII. It was the finishing touch to the basilica, which had been completed a few decades earlier.

Bernini's challenge was to create a space that could hold vast crowds for papal blessings, frame the basilica grandly, and still feel welcoming. His solution, the elliptical colonnade, is considered one of the finest public spaces ever built.

What to See in the Square

A few features are worth stopping for:

  • The colonnade - Two semicircular rows of columns, 284 in all, four columns deep, topped by 140 statues of saints looking down on the square. Stand on one of the two round paving stones set into the ground, and the four rows of columns line up to look like a single row.
  • The obelisk - The ancient Egyptian obelisk at the centre is over 4,000 years old. It was brought to Rome in the first century and moved to this spot in 1586, a huge engineering feat for its day. It has no hieroglyphs, unlike most Egyptian obelisks.
  • The two fountains - On either side of the obelisk stand matching fountains, one by Maderno and one added later by Bernini to balance it.

The Pope in St. Peter's Square

The square is where the public sees the Pope. On most Wednesdays he gives the General Audience here, weather permitting, and on Sundays he appears at a window of the Apostolic Palace to lead the midday Angelus prayer and blessing. These are free to attend, though the Wednesday audience needs a free ticket in advance.

At Christmas and Easter, the Pope gives the Urbi et Orbi blessing from the central balcony of the basilica to the crowds filling the square. Our general papal audience guide explains how to attend, and our Christmas at the Vatican guide covers the festive events.

Visiting the Square

St. Peter's Square is free and open, and there is no ticket to stand in it. A few tips to get the most from it:

  • Go early or late - The square is at its calmest and most photogenic early in the morning or in the evening light, before and after the daytime crowds.
  • Pair it with the basilica - Most people cross the square to visit the free basilica, so allow time for both. Our St. Peter's Basilica guide covers what to see inside.
  • Mind the Wednesday audience - On Wednesday mornings the square fills with seating and crowds for the papal audience, which is wonderful to see but means limited access.

Many visitors pair the square and basilica with the Vatican Museums and Sistine Chapel next door. The easiest way to do that without long queues is to book a timed skip-the-line entry ticket for the museums, then walk round to the square and basilica, which are free.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is St. Peter's Square?

It is the large oval plaza in front of St. Peter's Basilica in Vatican City, designed by Bernini and built between 1656 and 1667. It is ringed by a colonnade and used for papal blessings and big ceremonies.

Where is St. Peter's Square?

St. Peter's Square is in Vatican City, directly in front of the basilica, within Rome. It is open to everyone, with the nearest metro at Ottaviano-San Pietro on Line A.

Who designed St. Peter's Square?

Gian Lorenzo Bernini, the leading artist of Baroque Rome. He designed the elliptical colonnade to frame the basilica and welcome crowds, working from 1656 to 1667.

Do I need a ticket for St. Peter's Square?

No. The square is free and open to all, with no ticket and no border check. Security screening only applies when you go on to enter the basilica.

What is the obelisk in St. Peter's Square?

An ancient Egyptian obelisk, over 4,000 years old, brought to Rome in the first century and set up in the square in 1586. It stands at the centre, between the two fountains.

Can I see the Pope in St. Peter's Square?

Yes. The Pope gives the Wednesday General Audience in the square and the Sunday Angelus from a palace window, both free, and the Urbi et Orbi blessing at Christmas and Easter.
