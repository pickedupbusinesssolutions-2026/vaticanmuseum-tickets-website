How Much Are Vatican Museums Tickets

The official full-price ticket is €20. It went up from €17 at the start of 2024 and has stayed at €20 for 2026. That price covers timed entry to the Vatican Museums and the Sistine Chapel, with no guide.

On top of the €20, the official website charges a €5 booking fee to reserve a time slot, so the real online price is €25 per adult. You can in theory pay just €20 by buying at the ticket desk in person, but the desk often sells out and the queue to reach it can run one to two hours in busy months, so almost everyone books online and pays €25.

So when you see "€20" quoted, read it as €25 once you have booked a slot you can use. Everything above that, skip-the-line tickets, audio guides, and tours, is an extra you choose to add.

Who Gets Reduced or Free Entry

Not everyone pays full price. The reduced and free categories are set by the Vatican, and you show proof at the entrance.

Reduced entry is €8, plus the €5 booking fee, for children aged 6 to 18 and for students up to the age of 25 who carry a valid student ID. You book the reduced ticket in advance the same way as a full one, and you bring the ID that matches it.

Free entry applies to children under 6, who still need to be booked onto a ticket even though they pay nothing, and to ICOM card holders.

Everyone gets in free on the last Sunday of the month, which we cover below. If you are travelling as a family, our visiting with kids guide has more on booking for children.

Why Skip-the-Line Tickets and Tours Cost More

If the official ticket is €25, why do so many tickets cost more? Because you are paying for something extra on top of entry.

A skip-the-line ticket from a reseller, from about €33, buys you a guaranteed time slot and an instant mobile ticket when the official site is sold out. You pay a little more than €25 for the availability and the convenience.

A guided tour from €125 adds a live guide, the Sistine Chapel, and a walk into St. Peter's Basilica through the internal shortcut. The premium options, like early morning, breakfast, and private tours, cost more again for early access, a meal, or a guide to yourselves.

None of these are the Vatican charging more. They are separate companies bundling the €20 entry with a service. That is normal, and it is often worth it, but it is why prices range from €25 to over €200. Free Entry on the Last Sunday

The Vatican Museums are free for everyone on the last Sunday of each month, open from 9:00 am to 2:00 pm with the last entry at 12:30 pm. It is the one day you pay nothing at all.

The catch is that it is the busiest day of the month. You cannot book a skip-the-line ticket for it, so you queue with everyone else, and the wait can be long.

It suits budget travellers with time and patience, not anyone on a tight schedule. Our free entry on the last Sunday guide explains how to make the most of it.

Is It Worth Paying More Than €25

For many visitors, yes. The €25 official ticket is the cheapest reliable way in, but it gets you timed entry and nothing else, and it sells out early. Paying a bit more for a skip-the-line ticket makes sense when the official slots are gone.

Paying for a guided tour makes sense if you want someone to explain a collection this size and to take you into St. Peter's Basilica without the second security line.

It is not worth paying more if you are on a budget, happy to explore on your own, and can book the official ticket early. In that case €25 is all you need. Work out which group you are in before you book, and do not pay for a guide or early access you will not use.

How to Book Without Overpaying

A few simple rules keep the cost down.

Book early and direct if you can. The official €25 ticket is the cheapest reliable option, so if your dates are set, book it as far ahead as possible before it sells out. If it is gone, a skip-the-line ticket is the next best value.

Only add what you will use. The audio guide is €8 and worth it if you want context on your own. A full guided tour is worth more only if you want the guide and St. Peter's. Do not buy a premium tour for features you will not use.

Avoid the street sellers near the Vatican who offer to skip the line, and book online with a seller that gives you an instant mobile ticket and free cancellation instead. The skip-the-line entry ticket below is a reliable option when the official site is full.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a Vatican Museums ticket cost in 2026?

The official full ticket is €20, plus a €5 online booking fee, so €25 in total when you book online. Reduced entry is €8 for ages 6 to 18 and students under 25, and children under 6 are free.

Why is the online price €25 and not €20?

The €20 is the entry price, and the official website adds a €5 fee to reserve your time slot, making €25. You can pay €20 at the ticket desk in person, but it often sells out and the queue is long, so most people book online.

Are Vatican Museums tickets ever free?

Yes. Entry is free for everyone on the last Sunday of the month, from 9:00 to 14:00, and it is always free for children under 6 and ICOM card holders. The free Sunday is the busiest day and has no skip-the-line.

How much is a Vatican guided tour?

Guided tours start from about €125 for a standard group tour that includes a live guide, the Sistine Chapel, and St. Peter's Basilica. Early morning, breakfast, and private tours cost more.

Why do some Vatican tickets cost over €30?

Those are skip-the-line tickets or tours sold by other companies that bundle the €20 entry with a guaranteed slot, a mobile ticket, a guide, or early access. The extra pays for the service, not for the Vatican charging more.

Do children and students pay less?

Yes. Children aged 6 to 18 and students under 25 pay the reduced €8 entry with valid ID, plus the €5 booking fee. Children under 6 are free but still need a booked ticket.

What is the cheapest way to visit the Vatican Museums?

The free last Sunday of the month if you can handle the crowds, or the official €25 online ticket booked early. A reseller skip-the-line ticket from about €33 is the next cheapest reliable option when the official site is sold out.
