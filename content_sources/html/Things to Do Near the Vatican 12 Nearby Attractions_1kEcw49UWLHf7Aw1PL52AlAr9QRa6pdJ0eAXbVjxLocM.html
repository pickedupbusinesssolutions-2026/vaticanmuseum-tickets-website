Things to Do Near the Vatican: 12 Nearby Attractions

Quick answer: The best things to do near the Vatican are Castel Sant'Angelo and the Ponte Sant'Angelo, both a short walk away, the panoramic Gianicolo hill, the lively neighbourhood of Trastevere, and a cluster of famous sights across the river, including Piazza Navona, the Pantheon, and Campo de' Fiori. Most are a 10 to 30 minute walk, while the Trevi Fountain and Spanish Steps are a little further or a short metro ride. They pair perfectly with a morning at the Vatican Museums.

Things to do near the Vatican are easy to line up, because the Vatican sits right on the edge of central Rome, just across the river from many of the city's most famous sights.

Once you have seen the St. Peter's Basilica and St. Peter's Square, you can walk to a castle, a great viewpoint, and some of Rome's best squares in minutes.

This guide lists 12 nearby attractions, roughly from the closest outward, with how to reach each one.

The 12 Attractions

1. Castel Sant'Angelo

The round fortress on the river, about a 10 to 15 minute walk from St. Peter's along Via della Conciliazione. Built as the Emperor Hadrian's tomb and later a papal castle, it has great views from the top and a secret passage linking it to the Vatican.

2. Ponte Sant'Angelo

The pedestrian bridge in front of the castle, lined with ten angel statues designed by Bernini. It is one of the prettiest spots in Rome, especially at sunset, and it is right on the way between the Vatican and the city centre.

3. The Gianicolo (Janiculum Hill)

A short uphill walk of about 20 minutes gives you the best free panorama of Rome, looking out over the whole historic centre. A cannon is fired here every day at noon. It is quiet, green, and far less crowded than the city's other viewpoints.

4. Trastevere

The charming old neighbourhood across the river, about a 20 to 30 minute walk or a short tram ride. Its narrow cobbled lanes are full of restaurants, bars, and the lovely church of Santa Maria in Trastevere. Our where to eat near the Vatican guide has local food spots.

5. Via Cola di Rienzo

The main shopping street of the Prati district, just a few minutes from the Vatican. It is where locals shop, with clothing stores, cafes, and the famous Castroni and Franchi food halls, making it handy for a break or a picnic.

6. Piazza Navona

Rome's grandest Baroque square, about a 20 to 25 minute walk across the river. It is built on an ancient stadium and centred on Bernini's dramatic Fountain of the Four Rivers, ringed by cafes and street artists.

7. The Pantheon

The best-preserved building of ancient Rome, roughly a 25 to 30 minute walk away. Its vast domed ceiling, open to the sky through a central hole, is one of the most impressive sights in the city, and entry is quick and cheap.

8. Campo de' Fiori

A busy square with a morning market of produce and flowers, turning into a lively nightlife spot after dark. It is close to Piazza Navona, so the two are easy to pair on the same walk.

9. The Ara Pacis Museum

A modern glass museum on the riverbank holding the Ara Pacis, a beautifully carved ancient Roman altar of peace. It is a calm, air-conditioned stop and sits near the Mausoleum of Augustus.

10. Piazza del Popolo

A huge oval square at the northern edge of the old centre, with an Egyptian obelisk, twin churches, and a stairway up to the Pincio terrace for another fine view. The metro stop Flaminio is right beside it.

11. The Spanish Steps

The famous sweep of steps leading up to the church of Trinità dei Monti, a 30 to 35 minute walk or a short metro ride to Spagna. The streets around it are Rome's main luxury shopping district.

12. The Trevi Fountain

Rome's most famous fountain, a theatrical wall of carved figures and falling water where visitors toss a coin to ensure a return to Rome. It is about a 30 to 35 minute walk from the Vatican, or a short hop by metro and foot.

How to Fit Them In

The easiest plan is to do the Vatican in the morning, when it is quietest, then spend the afternoon working your way back through these sights toward the centre. A natural route is to leave the Vatican, cross at Castel Sant'Angelo and the Ponte Sant'Angelo, then walk on to Piazza Navona, the Pantheon, and the Trevi Fountain, seeing most of the list in one afternoon.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is there to do near the Vatican?

The closest attractions are Castel Sant'Angelo and the Ponte Sant'Angelo, the Gianicolo viewpoint, and the Prati shopping street, with Trastevere, Piazza Navona, the Pantheon, and Campo de' Fiori a short walk across the river.

What is the closest attraction to the Vatican?

Castel Sant'Angelo, about a 10 to 15 minute walk along Via della Conciliazione, with the angel-lined Ponte Sant'Angelo right in front of it.

Can I walk from the Vatican to the city centre?

Yes. It is a pleasant 20 to 30 minute walk from St. Peter's across the river to Piazza Navona and the Pantheon, passing Castel Sant'Angelo on the way.

Is the Colosseum near the Vatican?

No. The Colosseum is on the other side of central Rome, about 40 minutes away by metro or a longer walk. It is best done as a separate trip or on a combo tour.

What should I do after visiting the Vatican Museums?

Head toward the river and see Castel Sant'Angelo, then walk on to Piazza Navona, the Pantheon, and the Trevi Fountain. Doing the Vatican in the morning leaves the afternoon free for these.

Where is the best view near the Vatican?

The Gianicolo, or Janiculum Hill, a short uphill walk away, has the best free panorama over Rome, along with a cannon fired every day at noon.
