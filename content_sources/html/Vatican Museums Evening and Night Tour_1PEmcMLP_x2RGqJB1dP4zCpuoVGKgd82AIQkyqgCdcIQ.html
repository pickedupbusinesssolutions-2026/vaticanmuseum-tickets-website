Vatican Museums Evening and Night Tour

Quick answer: A Vatican night tour lets you visit the Vatican Museums and the Sistine Chapel after dark, with far fewer people than in the day. Crowds drop by around half to two thirds, the galleries are cooler, and you still see the Raphael Rooms and the Sistine Chapel. No night tour includes St. Peter's Basilica, because it closes in the evening.

A Vatican night tour is not one fixed tour but a few evening options, and this guide helps you pick the right one.

There is a standard Vatican evening tour, a small group tour, and a private tour, all seeing the Vatican Museums and Sistine Chapel after the day crowds have gone.

They are similar in what they cover, so the real choice is about group size, budget, and whether you need step-free access.

This guide lines up the three side by side, explains when the night tours run, and covers the one thing that surprises people, which is that the Basilica is not part of an evening visit.

Which Evening or Night Tour to Choose

The three tours see the same sights, so pick on group size, budget, and access.

  • Best value, happy in a normal group: The standard Vatican Evening Tour does the job at the lowest price.
  • Want it quieter and more personal: The Small Group tour caps the group at six, so the halls feel calmer and you can talk to your guide.
  • Travelling as a private party, or need step-free access: The Private Tour is the only one that is wheelchair accessible, and it moves entirely at your pace.

Why Visit the Vatican at Night

Seeing the Vatican at night is a completely different experience from the daytime rush.

The Vatican limits how many tickets it sells for the evening, so foot traffic drops by around 50 to 70 percent compared with the midday peak. That means quieter corridors, room to stand and look, and a much calmer Sistine Chapel.

The other wins are simple. Summer evenings in Rome are cooler than the middle of the day, so you are not queuing in the heat.

The galleries look different under the warm evening lighting, especially the gold ceiling of the Gallery of Maps. And the whole pace feels slower, which suits a first visit far better than being pushed along in a crowd.

If an early start suits you better than a late one, the other quiet option is an early morning tour, which gets you in before the public instead.

Why there is no St. Peter's Basilica at night?

This is the one thing a lot of people get wrong, so let us be clear. No Vatican night or evening tour includes St. Peter's Basilica.

The Basilica closes to the public in the early evening, between 6:30 and 7:00 pm, and the internal corridor that links the Sistine Chapel straight into the Basilica is locked at night.

Every night tour therefore ends inside the Sistine Chapel or the museum galleries, and you leave through the main Vatican Museums exit near the Bramante spiral staircase onto Viale Vaticano.

If seeing the Basilica matters to you, plan it for a separate morning or daytime visit. A daytime Vatican Museums guided tour does include it, and our St. Peter's Basilica tickets guide covers visiting it on its own.

What you see on this Vatican Night Tour

The route is the same one the daytime tours follow, just calmer and under evening light.

You start in the Pinecone Courtyard, with its giant Roman bronze pinecone and the modern Sphere Within Sphere sculpture, then move through the Pio-Clementino Museum, home to the Apollo Belvedere and the Laocoön.

From there you walk the three great upper galleries, the Gallery of the Candelabra, the Gallery of Tapestries, and the Gallery of Maps, before reaching the Raphael Rooms and Raphael's famous The School of Athens.

The tour finishes at the Sistine Chapel, where the guide explains the ceiling and The Last Judgment on panels outside first, since silence is enforced inside.

Rules, Dress Code, and Tips

A few things to know before an evening visit.

  • You still pass through security. Even with a skip-the-line pass, everyone clears airport-style metal detectors at the Viale Vaticano entrance, which takes around 15 to 20 minutes.
  • The dress code applies at night too. Shoulders and knees must be covered, for men and women. No sleeveless tops, shorts, short skirts, or flip-flops. See the Vatican dress code guide.
  • Photography rules. No flash in the galleries, and no photography or video at all inside the Sistine Chapel.
  • Bring ID. The lead booker's name is checked at security, so carry a passport or government photo ID that matches the booking.
  • Bring a light layer. Rome's evenings are warm, but the climate control inside the galleries and the Sistine Chapel can feel cool.

Frequently Asked Questions

What time do the evening tours start?

Entries usually begin between 6:00 and 7:30 PM, with last entry at 8:00 PM, and the museums stay open until about 10:00 to 10:30 PM. The exact start time for your tour is shown when you book.

Does a Vatican night tour include St. Peter's Basilica?

No. St. Peter's Basilica closes in the evening and the connecting corridor is locked, so no night or evening tour includes it. Visit the Basilica on a separate daytime trip instead.

Is the Vatican quieter at night?

Yes. The Vatican limits evening tickets, so crowds are around 50 to 70 percent lower than the midday peak. That is the main reason to go at night.

Do the night tours include the Sistine Chapel and Raphael Rooms?

Yes. All three tours cover the Raphael Rooms and finish at the Sistine Chapel, along with the main galleries.

Are the night tours wheelchair accessible?

Only the Private Tour is. It uses a step-free route, while the standard and small group tours follow stair-heavy paths that are not wheelchair accessible.
