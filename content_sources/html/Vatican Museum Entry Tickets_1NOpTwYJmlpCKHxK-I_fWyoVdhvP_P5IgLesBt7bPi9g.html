A skip-the-line ticket is a timed entry ticket to the Vatican Museums, and the Sistine Chapel comes with it. You pick a date and a time when you book, and that slot is your entry time.

When you arrive, you use the dedicated entrance rather than the general ticket queue, so the wait to get in is far shorter.

There is one thing worth being clear about, because it is where most of the confusion sits. Skipping the line means skipping the queue to buy and collect your ticket. It does not skip the security check.

Everyone who enters the Vatican Museums passes through airport-style security, and that queue is separate. More on that below, but keep it in mind so the timing on the day makes sense.

This is a self-guided ticket. There is no live guide walking you through the rooms. You go at your own pace, spend as long as you like, and leave when you are ready. If you would rather have someone explain what you are looking at, a Vatican guided tour is the better fit.

What the Vatican Entry Ticket includes

Here is what the standard entry ticket covers:

  • Skip-the-ticket-line entry to the Vatican Museums and the Sistine Chapel
  • Access to all the public galleries and halls that are open on the day
  • Help at the dedicated entrance desk so you get in smoothly
  • The booking fee, already included in the price
  • An audio guide in ten languages (Chinese, English, French, German, Italian, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese, Russian, and Spanish), which you collect at the desk inside
  • Free access to St. Peter's Basilica when it is open, though entry is not guaranteed and depends on crowd control
  • One thing to note before you book: this entry ticket is non-refundable once booked, and it is valid for one day, on your chosen date and time.

And here is what it does not cover:

  • A live tour guide, since this is a self-guided ticket
  • Climbing the dome (the cupola) at St. Peter's Basilica, which needs a separate ticket
  • Hotel pickup or any transport

How the Vatican Entry Works on the Day

The process is simple once you know the order of things.

Arrive at least 15 minutes before the time on your voucher. Head to the dedicated entrance and desk for ticket holders on Viale Vaticano, not the general line.

Show your voucher on your phone, collect your entry pass, and pick up your audio guide there if you booked one.

Next comes security. Everyone has to go through metal detectors and a bag check. This can take ten minutes, or more than thirty minutes when it is busy. A lot of people do not expect this, so give yourself extra time and do not arrive too late.

Once you are through, you are free. The route through the museums is one long, mostly one-way path that ends at the Sistine Chapel, so you can take it slowly and stop wherever you like until closing time.

What You Will See Inside the Museum

The Vatican Museums are not one museum but a whole run of galleries, and the ticket lets you walk the full public route. These are the highlights most people come for.

  • The Sistine Chapel, with Michelangelo's ceiling and The Creation of Adam, plus The Last Judgment on the altar wall. You can read more about it in our guide to the Sistine Chapel.
  • The Raphael Rooms and the Borgia Apartments, the papal living quarters covered in Renaissance frescoes, including The School of Athens. See the Raphael Rooms guide for detail.
  • The Gallery of Maps, a long corridor lined with 16th-century maps of Italy. Our Gallery of Maps page has more.
  • The Pio-Clementino Museum, home to famous ancient statues such as the Laocoön and the Apollo Belvedere.
  • The Greek Cross Gallery, the Cabinet of the Masks, and the Sala degli Animali, with carved sarcophagi and sculptures of real and mythical animals.

Because the Sistine Chapel sits at the very end of the route, you walk past most of these on the way to it. That is worth knowing when you plan your time inside.

The Dress Code and the Rules Inside

The Vatican is a religious site, and the dress code is enforced at the door. If you are not covered up, you can be turned away, so it is worth getting right.

Shoulders and knees must be covered, for men and women. That rules out shorts, short skirts, and sleeveless or vest tops.

A scarf or a light layer in your bag is an easy fix on a hot day. Our full Vatican dress code guide covers the details.

A few more rules to keep in mind:

  • Photography is allowed in the general galleries without flash or a tripod, but it is not allowed at all inside the Sistine Chapel. Staff will ask you to put your phone away there.
  • Large bags, suitcases, tripods, and long umbrellas have to go into the free cloakroom near the entrance, so travel light if you can.
  • Bring photo ID - The name on your ticket should match your passport or ID card.

Can you enter St. Peter's Basilica with this Entry Ticket?

This is a common question, so here is the honest version. St. Peter's Basilica is free to enter on its own, and it is not part of your museum ticket.

With this self-guided Vatican ticket, getting into the basilica afterwards is possible but not guaranteed, because access depends on crowd control on the day, and you will need to join the separate security queue in St. Peter's Square.

If seeing both the museums and the basilica in one smooth visit matters to you, look at a guided option, since some guided tours can lead you through an internal route that avoids the square queue. Our St. Peter's Basilica tickets guide explains the ways in.

When to Book and When to Go

Your ticket is for a specific date and time, not just a date, so the slot you pick is the slot you use. Popular times, especially mid-morning, go quickly, so book ahead rather than hoping for a same-day slot.

For a calmer visit, the first slot of the day or the last slots in the late afternoon tend to be quieter than the mid-morning rush.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a Vatican skip-the-line ticket include the Sistine Chapel?

Yes. The Sistine Chapel is part of the Vatican Museums route and is included with every entry ticket. You do not pay extra or book it separately.

How much is a Vatican Museum entry ticket?

This entry ticket with audio guide is €40 for adults (19 and over), €29 for youths aged 7 to 19, and free for children under 7. Prices can change, so confirm the current figure on the booking page.

Does skip-the-line mean I avoid all queues?

No. It lets you skip the queue to buy and collect your ticket, which is the long one outside. You still pass through security, and that check has its own line that can take ten to thirty minutes at busy times.

Can I get into St. Peter's Basilica with this entry ticket?

The basilica is free to enter but is not part of this ticket, and access after the museums is not guaranteed. You will usually queue separately in St. Peter's Square.

How long do you need to visit the Vatican Museums?

There is no fixed time on this ticket, so you decide. Most visitors spend about two to three hours inside, and you can stay until closing time. If you want to see the main highlights and the Sistine Chapel without rushing, plan for at least two hours.

What happens if I arrive late for my time slot?

Try not to be late, as your ticket is for a set date and time. If you are only a little late, staff will often still let you in, especially at quieter times, but this is not guaranteed.

Which days are the Vatican Museums closed?

The museums are open most days but close on most Sundays, except the last Sunday of the month. They also close on a few religious holidays each year. Dates can change, so check the current days on our opening hours guide before you book.

Are there toilets, cafes, and a cloakroom inside?

Yes. There are toilets in several spots along the route, cafes and food points for a drink or a snack, and a free cloakroom near the entrance where you leave large bags, suitcases, and umbrellas.

Can I buy the Vatican entry ticket at the entrance instead of online?

You can buy tickets at the door, but that means joining the long ticket line outside, which is exactly what this ticket helps you avoid. Booking online gives you a set entry time and lets you use the ticket holders' entrance, so it is the faster and safer choice in busy months.

Is the Vatican Museum entry ticket good for young children and strollers?

Yes. Children under 7 enter free, and families are welcome. You can bring a stroller, though during very busy periods you may be asked to fold it or leave a large one at the cloakroom. Remember to carry ID for children, as staff may ask to see it.
