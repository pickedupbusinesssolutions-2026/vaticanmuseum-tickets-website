Vatican Private Tour
Quick answer: A Vatican private tour gives you a licensed guide reserved just for your group, skip-the-line entry, and the freedom to set your own pace through the Vatican Museums, the Sistine Chapel, and St. Peter's Basilica. There are three main options: Vatican private tour, a semi-private tour, and an early morning private tour. Prices run from about €159 for the semi-private tour up to around €700 for a full private tour. Only the full private tour is wheelchair accessible, and all three include St. Peter's Basilica. 


A Vatican private tour is the way to see the Vatican on your own terms, with a guide who works only for your group. 
Instead of following a fixed route with 25 strangers, you decide where to slow down, what to skip, and when to take a break. 
This private tour suits families with kids, art lovers, people with mobility or hearing needs, and anyone who wants a route built around their interests rather than a fixed group walk.
There is more than one kind of private tour, though, and they are not the same. This guide compares the three main options, what each one gives you, where they meet, and the fine print that trips people up, so you can pick the right one and book with confidence.

Why Book a Private Vatican Tour
A standard group tour follows one fixed, non-stop route. A private guide changes that in a few real ways.
* Your pace and your route - You can spend extra time where you want, such as the Raphael Rooms, the Pinacoteca art gallery, or the Egyptian Museum, and move quickly past the busy parts.
* A guide focused on your group - The commentary is built around what your party cares about, which is ideal for families with children, art history fans, or anyone with hearing or mobility needs.
* Break when you need them - A private guide can work in a short rest in the Pinecone Courtyard or a museum cafe without the group leaving you behind.
If you are happy in a normal group and want to save money, a standard Vatican Museums guided tour covers the same sights for less.
Meeting Points and Arrival
Each tour meets in a different spot, all near the Ottaviano stop on Metro Line A. Arrive about 15 minutes early for identity and voucher checks, as tours leave on time.
* Vatican Private Tour: Outside the Vatican Museums entrance steps at Viale Vaticano 100. Your private guide holds a name board or company sign.
* VIP Semi-Private Tour: Inside the supplier office at Via Germanico 16, a 5-minute walk from Ottaviano metro. Check in 15 minutes before departure.
* Early Morning Private Tour: Via Tunisi 4, across the street from the main museum entrance steps, near Caffè Vaticano.

What you see on the Tour
A private tour covers the same highlights as any full Vatican tour, just at your pace. You walk through the Pinecone Courtyard, the upper galleries, and the 120-metre Gallery of Maps, with time in the Raphael Rooms too.
The route builds to the Sistine Chapel, with Michelangelo's ceiling and The Last Judgment, and then most tours pass directly into St. Peter's Basilica to see Michelangelo's Pietà and Bernini's Baldacchino. 
With a private guide, you can also ask for extra time in spots a group tour skips, such as the Pinacoteca or the Egyptian Museum.
Rules and Fine Print
A few things to sort out before you go, because the Vatican is strict about them.
* Every traveller's name is needed at booking. The Vatican checks names against the ticket, so give the full legal first and last name for everyone, and bring a passport or photo ID that matches exactly. Security will refuse entry if the names do not match.
* Dress code is enforced. Shoulders and knees must be covered, for men and women. No sleeveless tops, shorts, or short skirts. See the Vatican dress code guide.
* You still pass security. Priority entry skips the ticket line, not the metal detectors, which can take 15 to 30 minutes in high season.
* Travel light to avoid the exit walk. Bags over about 40 by 35 by 15 cm, large backpacks, hard luggage, long umbrellas, and tripods must go to the cloakroom. Since tours end at St. Peter's Square, anything left at the museum cloakroom means a 15 to 20 minute walk back around the walls. Bring only a small bag you can carry the whole way.
* Wednesday mornings are different. The Pope holds the Papal Audience in St. Peter's Square, and the Basilica is closed to tour groups, so your guide spends the time on extra galleries such as the Etruscan collection or Borgia Apartments instead. See our General Papal Audience guide.
Frequently Asked Questions
What is the difference between a private and a semi-private Vatican tour?
A private tour is booked for your group only, so no strangers join. A semi-private, or VIP, tour is a small shared group with a low cap, which keeps it personal but costs less than a fully private guide.
Is a Vatican private tour worth it?
A private Vatican tour is worth it if you want to set your own pace, focus on what interests you, or you are travelling with children or someone with mobility or hearing needs. If you are happy in a standard group, a normal guided tour costs less for the same sights.
Does a private Vatican tour include St. Peter's Basilica?
Yes. All three options here include St. Peter's Basilica, either as a guided walk or, on the early morning tour, as an optional add-on. The dome climb is not included and needs a separate ticket.
How far ahead should I book a private tour?
Book the private tour as early as you can. Private and small group slots are limited and sell out, especially in peak season and around the early morning slots.
What do I need to bring?
A passport or photo ID matching the name on your booking, clothing that meets the dress code, and only a small bag. Everyone in your party needs their full name on the booking for Vatican security.
Can I customize what we see on the private tour?
Yes. Because the guide works only for your group, you can ask for extra time in areas like the Pinacoteca art gallery, the Egyptian Museum, or the Raphael Rooms, and move quickly past the parts that interest you less.
What happens if my tour falls on a Wednesday?
On Wednesday mornings, the Pope holds the Papal Audience and St. Peter's Basilica is closed to tour groups. On those days your guide spends the extra time inside the Vatican Museums instead, covering more galleries such as the Etruscan collection or the Borgia Apartments.
