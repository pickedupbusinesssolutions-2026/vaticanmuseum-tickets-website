Vatican Dress Code
Quick answer: The Vatican dress code comes down to one rule: your shoulders and knees must be covered. That means no sleeveless or low-cut tops, no shorts above the knee, no miniskirts, and no hats inside. It applies to everyone, men and women, and across the whole Vatican, the Museums, the Sistine Chapel, and St. Peter's Basilica. It is enforced at every entrance, and you will be turned away if you break it, so carry a light scarf or shawl to cover up if you need to.


Getting the Vatican dress code wrong is one of the most common ways visitors lose their morning, turned away at the door after queuing, because a guard decides a top or a pair of shorts does not pass. 
The good news is the rule is simple and the same everywhere in the Vatican, so once you know it you can dress for the whole visit in one go.
This guide gives you the exact rule, what it means for women and for men, the shoe and hat rules, where it is strictest, and how to fix it on the spot if you are caught out.

The Vatican Dress Code Rule

The official rule is that entry is only for appropriately dressed visitors, and it bans sleeveless or low-cut tops, shorts above the knee, miniskirts, and hats. In plain terms: keep your shoulders and your knees covered, and take your hat off inside.
That one principle, shoulders and knees covered, is all you really need to remember. It applies to the Vatican Museums, the Sistine Chapel, and St. Peter's Basilica alike, since they are all sacred or church-run spaces. Clothing with offensive images or slogans is also not allowed, and the same goes for anything the staff judge to break the decorum of the place.
Dress Code for Women
For women, the rule plays out like this:
* Tops: Cover your shoulders. No sleeveless tops, vest tops, or spaghetti straps, and nothing low-cut or see-through. Short sleeves are fine. A crop top that shows the midriff is not.
* Bottoms: Skirts, dresses, and trousers must reach the knee or below. Miniskirts and short shorts are not allowed. Leggings and long trousers are fine, as they cover the knee.
* Easy options: A maxi dress or midi skirt, or trousers with a short-sleeved top, pass without a second thought. If you want a sleeveless dress, bring a shawl or light cardigan to cover your shoulders inside.
Dress Code for Men
For men, it is just as straightforward:
* Tops: A t-shirt or shirt with sleeves is fine. Vests, tank tops, and going shirtless are not. Avoid offensive slogans or graphics.
* Bottoms: Long trousers are the safe choice. Shorts are only acceptable if they reach the knee, so anything above the knee will be refused. Many men just wear lightweight long trousers in summer to avoid the issue.
* Hats: Take any hat or cap off before you go inside.
Dress Code for Children
Teenagers are treated the same as adults, so from roughly the teens up they need covered shoulders and knees, with no short shorts or vest tops. 
For young children and toddlers the rule is applied much more leniently, and little ones in sleeveless summer clothes or shorts are not usually stopped.
Even so, it is worth dressing children in covered, comfortable clothes, partly to be safe at the strict Basilica door and partly because the galleries are crowded and the walk is long. 
Keep a light layer in your bag to cover a child's shoulders if staff ask. Our visiting with kids guide has more on bringing children.


Shoes, Hats, and Bags
There is no strict shoe rule, but comfort matters because you will walk for hours on marble floors. Closed, flat, comfortable shoes are the best choice. Sandals are allowed, but beach flip-flops look too casual and are best avoided.
Hats must come off inside the Museums, the Sistine Chapel, and the Basilica, though a hat is useful for the outdoor queue in summer. Large bags, backpacks, and cases over about 40 by 35 by 15 cm, along with tripods and long umbrellas, have to go in the free cloakroom near the entrance, which our things to know before you go guide covers.
Where the Dress Code Is Strictest
The rule is the same across the Vatican, but it is enforced most tightly at St. Peter's Basilica, where guards at the door check everyone and turn away anyone with bare shoulders or knees, with no exceptions. 
The Sistine Chapel and the Museums use the same standard, checked at the Museums entrance as you go through security.
If you are attending a Papal Audience in St. Peter's Square, the setting is outdoors and a little more relaxed, but you are still expected to dress modestly, so the same shoulders-and-knees guidance applies. Our St. Peter's Basilica tickets and General Papal Audience guides have more on each.
What to Do If You're Not Dressed Right
If you turn up in shorts or a sleeveless top, you will not be let in until you cover up, and the Vatican does not lend out cover-ups. Your options are simple:
* Carry a scarf or shawl - A large light scarf is the easiest fix, it covers either your shoulders or, tied as a wrap, your knees. Keep one in your bag just in case.
* Buy one from a street vendor - Sellers outside the entrance sell scarves, shawls, and thin cover-ups for around €2 to €15, but they cost more than buying ahead and you are at their mercy.
* Plan ahead - The best fix is to dress right in the morning so you never have to think about it, especially if your visit includes the Basilica.
Dressing for the Heat
Rome summers are hot, often over 30°C, and the rule does not relax for the weather, so the trick is to cover up in light fabrics. 
Choose breathable cotton or linen in loose cuts: a maxi dress, a midi skirt, or light long trousers with a short-sleeved top keep you cool while meeting the rule. Avoid sheer fabrics, which do not count as covered.
Pack a light scarf you can throw on, wear a hat for the outdoor queue and take it off inside, and choose comfortable shoes. Once you are dressed and ready, book a timed skip-the-line entry ticket so you are not standing in the sun longer than you have to.
Frequently Asked Questions
What is the Vatican dress code?
Your shoulders and knees must be covered. That rules out sleeveless or low-cut tops, shorts above the knee, miniskirts, and hats inside. It applies to everyone across the Museums, Sistine Chapel, and St. Peter's Basilica, and it is enforced at the entrances.
Can I wear shorts to the Vatican?
Only if they reach the knee. Shorts above the knee are not allowed and you will be refused entry. In summer, lightweight long trousers or knee-length shorts are the safe choice.
Can women wear trousers or leggings?
Yes. Trousers and leggings are fine because they cover the knee. The rule for women is about covering the shoulders and knees, not about skirts versus trousers.
Does the dress code apply to men?
Yes, equally. Men need covered shoulders (a t-shirt or shirt with sleeves, not a vest) and bottoms that reach the knee, and must remove hats inside.
Is the dress code enforced?
Yes, strictly, especially at St. Peter's Basilica, where guards check at the door and turn away anyone not covered up. The Museums and Sistine Chapel check at the main entrance.
What if I am not dressed properly?
You will not be let in until you cover up. Carry a light scarf or shawl to drape over your shoulders or knees, or buy one from a vendor outside for a few euros. The Vatican does not provide cover-ups.
Can I wear sandals to the Vatican?
Yes, sandals are allowed. Beach flip-flops are too casual and best if you avoid them. Closed comfortable shoes are the better choice for hours of walking on marble.
Do children have to follow the dress code?
The code is aimed at teens and adults and is applied more leniently to young children, but it is sensible to keep kids in covered, modest clothing too. See our visiting with kids guide.
