Most of the time, yes. The confusion comes from how the Vatican sells tickets. The official website releases a set number of timed entry slots, and once a day or a time fills up, it shows as sold out. That does not mean the museum is full. 
It means that one booking channel has used up its share, and the cheap entry tickets are the first to go, often one to two months ahead.
Tour companies and ticket resellers buy their own blocks of tickets in advance, and those run on a separate supply. So when the official site has nothing for tomorrow, a hosted entry or a guided tour from another seller is often still available for the same day. 
That is why last-minute visitors almost always find a way in, even in peak season. You will pay a bit more than a basic gate ticket, because the price bundles in the hosted escort or the live guide, but you get a guaranteed way through the door.

How Late Can You Book
Later than you would think. The hosted entry and the tours can often be booked the day before or the morning of your visit, as long as slots remain. 
The hosted entry in particular runs on frequent half-hourly and hourly departures, so it is the most likely to have space at short notice. Availability is just tighter, and prices can be a little higher than booking well ahead.
A few timing points help:
* Same day works, but early is safer. Booking the night before locks in a morning slot before the day fills up.
* Popular times go first. Mid-morning slots sell out before late afternoon ones, so if you are flexible, a later slot is easier to grab.
* Confirmation is instant. All three options send a mobile ticket straight away, so you can book and go.
What to Do If Everything Is Sold Out
If your first choice shows nothing for your dates, do not give up. Try these in order:
* Check a later time or the next day. Afternoon slots and the following morning often have space even when your first choice is gone.
* Switch from the hosted entry to a tour. The guided and private tours hold a separate ticket pool, so one can be open when the hosted entry is full, and the reverse.
* Try the last Sunday of the month. The Vatican Museums are free on the last Sunday, from the morning until early afternoon. It is the busiest day and you cannot skip the line, but it costs nothing. See our free entry on the last Sunday's guide.
* Go as a walk-up as a last resort. You can queue to buy a ticket at the door, but the line can run one to two hours in high season, so treat this as the backup to everything else.
Tips for Booking Last Minute
When you are booking in a hurry, a few things make it safer:
* Pick free cancellation - All three options here can be cancelled up to 24 hours before, so a booking is not a gamble if your plans are still moving.
* Use the mobile ticket - Last minute means no time to collect or print anything, so the ticket going straight to your phone matters.
* Book the whole party under correct names - Vatican security checks that the name on the ticket matches your ID, so enter everyone correctly even when rushing.
* Check what is included - The hosted entry does not include St. Peter's Basilica or a guide, while the tours do, so read the inclusions before you pay.

Is there a free way into the Vatican?
