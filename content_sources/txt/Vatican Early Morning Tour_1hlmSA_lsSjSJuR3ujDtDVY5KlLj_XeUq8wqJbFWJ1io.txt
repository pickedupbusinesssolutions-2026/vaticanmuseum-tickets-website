Which Early Morning Tour Should You Choose
If you are after the best early morning Vatican tour for you, it comes down to budget and how much you want the Basilica guided.
* On a budget, or just want the early start: the standard Guided Tour is the best value. Just note the Basilica is self-guided at the end.
* Want a smaller group and a guided Basilica: the Small Group tour is the sweet spot, with time in the Raphael Rooms too.
* Want it quiet and flexible, or travelling as a family or small party: the Private Tour gives you 6 people or fewer and the most time inside the Basilica.
* Want something special: the Breakfast Tour lets you eat inside the Vatican grounds before the crowds, though it cannot be cancelled.
All four include the Sistine Chapel and skip-the-line entry, so you are really choosing on group size, the Basilica, and extras.
Why Take an Early Morning Vatican Tour
Standard tickets let the public in from about 8:00 to 9:00 am, and that is when thousands of people pour into the galleries at once. 
Early morning tours enter between 7:30 and 8:00 am, before the main gates open to everyone else. That head start is the whole point.
Going in early means three real things. You clear security before the big lines build. You walk the corridors, the Gallery of Maps, and the Raphael Rooms while they are still quiet. And you reach the Sistine Chapel with a fraction of the crowd you would face at midday, so you can stand still and look up without being pushed along.
If you only do one upgrade on your Vatican visit, an early start is the one most people say made the biggest difference. If a standard daytime slot suits you better, compare it with our Vatican Museums guided tour or a self-guided Vatican tour.


What you See on an Vatican Early Morning Tour
The early tours cover the same main sights as a daytime visit, just with more room to enjoy them. 
Depending on the tour, you walk through the Pinecone and Belvedere courtyards, the Gallery of Candelabra, the Gallery of Tapestries, and the Gallery of Maps, before reaching the Sistine Chapel.
The smaller and private tours also include the Raphael Rooms, the papal apartments painted by Raphael, which are one of the best reasons to go early since they get very tight later in the day. 
Most tours finish at or inside St. Peter's Basilica, home to Michelangelo's La Pietà and Bernini's Baldacchino.
Getting There and Where to Meet
Each tour has its own meeting point, listed in the details above, and they are all a short walk from the Ottaviano stop on Metro Line A. 
Piazza del Risorgimento and Viale Vaticano are both close to the museum entrance, so give yourself time to find the right spot and your tour's coordinator.
Arrive early for check-in, 15 to 20 minutes before your start time, as early tours leave promptly and latecomers cannot be added once the group has gone in.

Dress Code, Rules, and Essential Tips
A few things catch people out on early tours, so keep these in mind.
* The dress code applies even at 7:30 am. Shoulders and knees must be covered, for men and women. No shorts, short skirts, or sleeveless tops, or you will be turned away at security. See the Vatican dress code guide.
* You still pass through security. Early entry skips the ticket office, but everyone goes through airport-style metal detectors.
* Travel light. Large bags, luggage, and big backpacks are not allowed and go to the cloakroom. Since most tours end at St. Peter's Basilica, a bag left at the museum entrance means a walk back to collect it, so bring a small crossbody bag instead.
* Wednesday mornings are different. On Wednesdays the Pope holds the Papal Audience in St. Peter's Square, and the Basilica is closed to tour groups in the morning. On those days guides swap the Basilica time for more of the museums, such as the Pinacoteca. If you want to see the audience, read our General Papal Audience guide.
Frequently Asked Questions
What time does a Vatican early morning tour start?
Starting times depend on the tour. Early entries are from 7:30 to 8:20 am. The standard guided tour also offers an 11:00 slot. Exact times are shown on each ticket booking page.
Is an early morning Vatican tour worth it?
Yes, if you want to avoid the worst of the crowds. Entering before the public means quieter galleries and a much calmer Sistine Chapel, which is the single biggest complaint people have about a midday visit.
Do early morning tours include the Sistine Chapel?
Yes. Every early morning tour includes the Sistine Chapel, since it sits at the end of the museum route. The main differences between tours are group size and whether St. Peter's Basilica is guided.
Which Vatican early morning tour has the smallest group?
The Private Early Morning Tour is the smallest, capped at 6 people or available fully private. The Small Group tour is next, around 10 to 12 people.
Do I still go through security on an early tour?
Yes. Skip-the-line means you skip the ticket queue, not security. Everyone passes through metal detectors, though the lines are shorter early in the morning.
