What the Breakfast is Like
The breakfast is the part that makes these tours special, so here is what to expect. It is served inside the Vatican Museums in the Pinecone Courtyard, named after the giant Roman bronze pinecone at one end. 
You eat outdoors among the classical statues and the big modern bronze sphere in the middle, in a courtyard that is silent at that hour. If the weather is bad, breakfast moves inside to the museum cafeteria next door. 
On the two guided tours you get a full buffet. That means warm scrambled eggs, bacon or pancakes, croissants and Italian pastries, cheeses and cold cuts, cereals, yogurt, fresh fruit, and as much espresso, cappuccino, juice, and water as you want. It is a proper sit-down meal, not a snack.
On the audio tour the breakfast is lighter, a coffee or cappuccino with a pastry or a small breakfast box. You still eat inside the Vatican, but do not expect the full spread.
The Early Access Advantage
The early start is the real reason to book one of these tours. In peak season the Vatican Museums take over 25,000 visitors a day, and by late morning the galleries are shoulder to shoulder. The public doors open between 8:00 am and 9:00 am, and a breakfast tour gets you in before that. Here is how the morning runs:
* 7:45 to 8:15 am: you check in and clear security before the public arrives.
* 8:15 to 8:45 am: you eat breakfast in the quiet Pinecone Courtyard.
* 8:45 to 10:30 am: you walk the main galleries, the Gallery of Maps, the Tapestries, and the Gallery of Candelabra, while they are still calm.
* 10:30 to 11:00 am: you reach the Sistine Chapel before it fills up and gets loud.
That early window is the difference between seeing the ceiling in relative quiet and seeing it over a sea of heads. For a normal daytime visit, our skip-the-line entry ticket guide covers the standard options.
St. Peter's Basilica and the Shortcut
The two guided tours use a door most visitors never see. There is an internal passage that leads straight from the Sistine Chapel into St. Peter's Basilica, and it is open only to licensed guides with a group. Taking it saves you 20 to 30 minutes of walking and lets you skip the 1 to 2 hour public security line out in St. Peter's Square.
Two things to know. First, the shortcut is for guided groups only, so the €92 self-guided audio tour cannot use it. On that tour you leave through the main museum exit and join the public line for the basilica. 
Second, on Wednesdays St. Peter's Basilica is usually closed to tours in the morning because of the Papal Audience in the square, so guided breakfast tours on a Wednesday spend the extra time in the museum galleries instead. Is the Vatican Breakfast Tour Worth It
This is the question everyone asks, so here is a straight answer. The breakfast tour is worth it if you value getting in early and seeing the galleries and the Sistine Chapel without the crowds, and you like the idea of eating inside the Vatican as a one-off treat. That combination is hard to get any other way, and for a special trip it can be the highlight.
It is not worth it if you are mainly after the food. You are not paying €200 for breakfast, you are paying for the early access, and the meal is the extra. If you want the same quiet start for less money, a standard early morning tour gives you that without the breakfast. And if your budget is tight but you still want the experience, the audio tour gets you inside early and fed, just without a live guide or St. Peter's. 
So book a guided breakfast tour for a treat, pick the early morning tour if you only care about beating the crowds, and take the audio tour if you want the early access and breakfast on a budget.
Dress Code, ID and Rules
A few rules matter at the Vatican, and breakfast tours add one or two of their own.
The dress code is strict and enforced. Your shoulders and knees must be covered in the museums, the Sistine Chapel, and St. Peter's Basilica. No shorts, short skirts, or sleeveless tops, or you can be turned away with no refund. See our Vatican dress code guide for the detail.
Bring the passport or ID you booked with. Vatican security needs the names and birthdates of everyone in the group at booking, and the name on your ID must match the ticket exactly. Names cannot be changed later. 
Pack light too, as large bags, tripods, and metal-tipped umbrellas are not allowed, and everyone goes through a metal detector even on early entry.
Book well ahead. Courtyard seating is limited and the early tickets are capped, so these tours open for booking up to about 60 days out and sell out quickly in busy months. Note that the guided tours are non-refundable, so only book once your dates are fixed.
Frequently Asked Questions
Where is the breakfast served?
Breakfast is served inside the Vatican Museums, in the Pinecone Courtyard (Cortile della Pigna), an open courtyard with classical statues and a large modern bronze sphere. If the weather is bad, it moves into the museum cafeteria next door. 
What is included in the breakfast?
The two guided tours serve a full buffet, with eggs, bacon or pancakes, pastries, cheeses, cold cuts, fruit, yogurt, and unlimited coffee, cappuccino, and juice. The breakfast audio tour has a lighter Italian breakfast of coffee and a pastry.
What time does the tour start?
Check-in starts around 7:45 am. The early start is the whole point, as it gets you in before the public.
Does the breakfast tour include St. Peter's Basilica?
The two guided tours include St. Peter's Basilica, using the internal shortcut from the Sistine Chapel. The self-guided audio tour does not, because that shortcut is for guided groups only, so you would visit the basilica on your own.
Is the Vatican breakfast tour worth it?
The Vatican breakfast tour is worth it for the early, crowd-free access and the one-off experience of eating inside the Vatican, with the breakfast as a treat on top. If you only want to beat the crowds, a standard early morning tour costs less and does the same job.
Can I visit on a Wednesday?
You can, but St. Peter's Basilica is closed to tours on Wednesday mornings because of the Papal Audience. On those days guided breakfast tours spend the extra time in the museum galleries instead.
Are the breakfast tours refundable?
The two breakfast guided tours are non-refundable, with no date changes, because of the pre-booked early entry tickets. The audio tour offers free cancellation up to 24 hours before on some entry options, so check at checkout.
Is the breakfast tour good for kids?
It can be, since the early start means fewer crowds and a relaxed meal before the walking begins. Just note the full €200-plus guided price, and that under-18s pay reduced rates on standard tickets, so a normal early tour may suit families better on cost.
