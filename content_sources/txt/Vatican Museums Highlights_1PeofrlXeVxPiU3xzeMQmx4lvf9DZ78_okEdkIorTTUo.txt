The Top 10 Highlights
1. The Sistine Chapel
The climax of the whole visit and the reason most people come. Michelangelo's ceiling and his Last Judgment fill the final room of the museums, and standing under them is one of the great experiences in art. Our Sistine Chapel guide covers what to look for.
2. The Raphael Rooms
Four frescoed rooms painted by Raphael, including the School of Athens, which gathers the great philosophers of antiquity under a vast arch. They come shortly before the Sistine Chapel. See our Raphael Rooms guide.
3. The Gallery of Maps
A 120-metre corridor lined with 40 painted maps of Italy under a glowing golden ceiling. It is one of the most photographed rooms in the museums. Our Gallery of Maps guide has the detail.
4. Laocoön and His Sons
One of the most famous sculptures of the ancient world, showing a Trojan priest and his sons wrestling sea serpents. Rediscovered in Rome in 1506, it was the first work in the Vatican collection and still stuns visitors in the Octagonal Court.
5. The Apollo Belvedere
A graceful ancient marble of the god Apollo, standing near the Laocoön in the same courtyard. Nearby is the Belvedere Torso, a battered fragment of a muscular figure that Michelangelo admired and studied closely.
6. The Pinecone Courtyard
A large open courtyard named after a giant bronze pinecone from Roman times. In its centre sits Arnaldo Pomodoro's modern bronze Sphere Within Sphere, which slowly turns. It is a good place to pause and take a wide photo.
7. The Gallery of Tapestries
A long hall hung with huge Flemish tapestries woven from designs by Raphael's workshop. Look for the clever effects, where a figure's eyes and the painted floor seem to follow you as you walk past.
8. The Pinacoteca
The Vatican's painting gallery, slightly off the main route and much quieter. It holds Raphael's Transfiguration, a Caravaggio, and an unfinished Leonardo da Vinci, and it is well worth the short detour for anyone who loves paintings.
9. The Egyptian Museum
The Gregorian Egyptian Museum is a favourite with families, with real mummies, painted sarcophagi, and ancient statues. It is easy to skip in the rush to the Sistine Chapel, but it is one of the more memorable rooms.
10. The Spiral Staircase
The famous double-helix ramp at the exit, designed by Giuseppe Momo in 1932. Two spirals wind around each other so people going up and down never meet, and it makes the perfect final photo. See our spiral staircase guide.
How to See the Highlights
The highlights sit along the one-way route, ending at the Sistine Chapel, so you will pass most of them in order without backtracking. The exceptions are the Pinacoteca and the Egyptian Museum, which are slightly off to the side and need a small detour, so decide in advance if you want them.
A few simple choices make all the difference:
* Go at opening - The first entry of the day is the only time the Gallery of Maps and the Sistine Chapel are calm. Our best time to visit guide covers the quiet windows.
* Allow two to three hours - That is enough for the highlights without exhausting yourself, as our how long to spend guide explains.
* Follow a plan - The museum map shows how the route fits together, so you can decide what to prioritise.
* Consider a guide - On a guided tour, someone takes you straight to the best works and explains them, which saves time and adds a lot.
To see the highlights without queuing, book a timed skip-the-line entry ticket for an early slot.
Frequently Asked Questions
What are the must-see highlights of the Vatican Museums?
The Sistine Chapel, the Raphael Rooms with the School of Athens, the Gallery of Maps, the Laocoön and Apollo Belvedere sculptures, the Pinacoteca painting gallery, and the spiral staircase at the exit.
What should I see if I only have a couple of hours?
Head for the Gallery of Maps, the Raphael Rooms, and the Sistine Chapel, which are the core of the route, and finish at the spiral staircase. That covers the biggest highlights in about two hours.
Are all the highlights included in one ticket?
Yes. A standard Vatican Museums ticket covers the whole route, including the Sistine Chapel, the Raphael Rooms, the Gallery of Maps, and the sculpture galleries. There is no separate ticket for individual rooms.
What is the most famous thing in the Vatican Museums?
The Sistine Chapel, with Michelangelo's ceiling and Last Judgment, is the single most famous sight. The School of Athens in the Raphael Rooms and the Gallery of Maps are close behind.
Can I skip straight to the Sistine Chapel?
No. The museums are one long route, and the Sistine Chapel is the last room, so you walk through the galleries to reach it. That is why you pass most of the other highlights on the way.
