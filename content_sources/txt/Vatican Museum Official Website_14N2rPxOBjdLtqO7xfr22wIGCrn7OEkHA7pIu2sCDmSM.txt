What is the Official Vatican Museums Website?
The official website is museivaticani.va, run by the Vatican Museums themselves. The ".va" ending is Vatican City's own web address, which is the clearest sign a site is genuine. 
The main site has the opening hours, exhibitions, and visitor information, and its "Online Tickets" link sends you to the official ticket office at tickets.museivaticani.va, which is where you actually buy and pay.
So there are really two official addresses to know: museivaticani.va for information, and tickets.museivaticani.va for booking.
 Everything you buy there, including the standard ticket, already includes the Sistine Chapel at the end of the route, since the chapel is the last room of the museums rather than a separate attraction. Our Vatican Museums vs Sistine Chapel guide explains why there is only one ticket.

How to Book Tickets on the Official Website
Booking on the official site is straightforward once you are on the right page. Step by step:
1. Go to tickets.museivaticani.va, or open museivaticani.va and click "Online Tickets". Type the address yourself rather than clicking an ad.
2. Choose "Admission tickets" for the standard entry, or pick one of the Vatican tours.
3. Select your date and an available time slot.
4. Add the number of full and reduced tickets you need, and add the audio guide if you want one.
5. Enter each visitor's name. The name on the ticket should match the ID you bring, as this is now checked.
6. Pay by card. The €5 booking fee is added at this stage.
7. You get an e-ticket by email with a QR code. Show it on your phone at the entrance, no need to print.
Arrive about 15 minutes before your slot and join the line for your time. The ticket sets when you can enter, but you still pass through airport-style security. Check the opening hours so you pick a slot that suits your day.
When Tickets Are Released
The official website opens tickets about 60 days ahead. As soon as your dates fall inside that window, book, because the most wanted slots go first. In peak season, roughly April to June and September to October, the early morning slots at 8:00 and 8:30 am can sell out three to four weeks in advance.
If you are flexible, afternoon slots stay available longer, though the museums are busier later in the day. For the quietest combination of time and crowds, see our best time to visit guide.
What to Do When Your Date Is Sold Out
A "sold out" date on the official site is not always the end of it. Two things worth trying:
First, check again early in the morning Rome time. The official checkout can time out and release held slots back into the system, and cancellations tend to reappear, so a date that looked full can open up.
Second, and more reliably, the licensed booking platforms hold their own separate pools of tickets. A date that is gone on tickets.museivaticani.va is often still available through sellers like GetYourGuide and Viator, with skip-the-line entry and free cancellation, and sometimes with an audio guide or a live guide included. 
You pay a bit more than the €25 official price, but for a fixed travel date that cannot move, it is the difference between getting in and not.

Is the Official Website Safe? 
Yes, the official website is completely safe to buy from. The thing to watch is making sure you are actually on it. The real addresses end in ".va": museivaticani.va and tickets.museivaticani.va. 
Many other sites use names with "vatican" and "tickets" in them and look official at a glance, but they are resellers, not the Vatican's own office.
Buying from a licensed reseller is not a scam, and the big platforms are clear that they are a third party. The problem is the look-alike pages that imply they are official and charge a large markup, and the sellers who approach you near the entrance offering "skip-the-line" tickets, which you should ignore. 
To stay safe, type museivaticani.va into your browser yourself instead of clicking a sponsored search result, and only buy from the official site or a named, reputable platform. Our things to know before you go guide has more on avoiding the common traps.
Free Entry on the Last Sunday
On the last Sunday of each month the Vatican Museums are free to enter, from 9:00 am with the last entry at 12:30 pm. These free tickets are not sold on the official website and cannot be booked online at all. 
You simply turn up and queue, which can mean a long wait, as it is the busiest time of the month. Our free entry last Sunday guide lists the 2026 dates and how early to arrive.

Frequently Asked Questions
Is tickets.museivaticani.va the real site?
Yes. That is the official online ticket office of the Vatican Museums. You reach it directly or through the "Online Tickets" link on museivaticani.va.
How much are tickets on the official website?
A full adult ticket is €25 online, which is €20 plus a €5 booking fee. Reduced entry is €13 for ages 6 to 18 and students under 25, and children under 6 go free. An audio guide adds €8.
Why is there a €5 fee?
It is the online reservation fee for booking a timed slot in advance. The ticket itself is €20, and €5 is added when you book online. The on-site price is €20, but walk-up slots are limited and the queue is long.
How far in advance can I book?
Book about 60 days in advance. In peak season, the early morning slots can sell out three to four weeks ahead, so book as soon as your dates open up.
What should I do if my date is sold out?
Check the official site again early in the morning Rome time, as cancellations reappear. If it stays full, licensed platforms like GetYourGuide and Viator hold separate tickets and are often still available, with skip-the-line entry and free cancellation.
Can I buy free last-Sunday tickets online?
No. The free last-Sunday entry is walk-up only and is not sold on the official website or anywhere else. You turn up and queue.
Does the official website sell guided tours?
Yes, it offers a limited number of Vatican-led tours in small groups. There are far more tour options, times, and languages through licensed platforms if the official ones are full.
