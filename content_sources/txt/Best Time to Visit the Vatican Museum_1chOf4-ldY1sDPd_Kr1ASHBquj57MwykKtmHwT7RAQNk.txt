The best time to visit the Vatican is early in the morning or late in the afternoon, on a quiet weekday, in the low season. 
The Vatican Museums take over 25,000 visitors a day in peak months, and the difference between arriving at 8:00 am and arriving at 11:00 am is the difference between walking and shuffling.
This guide breaks it down in three ways: the best time of day, the best day of the week, and the best time of year, with a month-by-month crowd calendar. It also covers what to avoid and how to beat the crowds whenever you can actually go.

Two windows are quietest: the first hour after the 8:00 am opening, and the last two hours before the 8:00 pm close. The early slot is the single best choice, because you get ahead of the tour groups and reach the Sistine Chapel before it fills up and gets loud.
The busiest time is the middle of the day, roughly 10:00 am to 1:00 pm, when the tour groups and day-trippers all arrive together. 
If a morning slot is gone, a late afternoon entry around 4:00 pm to 5:00 pm is the next best thing, as the crowds thin out toward closing. 
Just remember the last entry is 6:00 pm and the halls start clearing 30 minutes before close, which our opening hours guide covers in full.
Best Day of the Week
Weekdays beat weekends, and the middle of the week is best of all. Tuesday, Wednesday, and Thursday are quieter than Monday, and much quieter than Saturday, which is the busiest day the museums are open.
Wednesday has a quirk worth knowing. On Wednesday mornings the Pope holds the Papal Audience in St. Peter's Square, which pulls some of the crowd away from the museums early in the day, so a Wednesday morning slot can be slightly calmer. 
The flip side is that once the audience ends, those visitors move on to the museums, so go early if you pick a Wednesday.
The one day to skip is the free last Sunday of the month. It is the single busiest slot of the month, the queues are long, and you cannot book a skip-the-line ticket, so unless free entry is the whole point of your visit, choose another day.

When to Avoid
A few times are worth steering clear of:
* Midday, 10:00 am to 1:00 pm - The fullest part of the day in every season.
* Saturdays - The busiest day of the week the museums are open.
* The free last Sunday of the month - Long queues, no skip-the-line, and the most crowded slot of the month. Our free entry on the last Sunday guide explains the trade-off.
* Easter and spring break (April) & the summer peak (May to September) - The highest crowds of the year.
* Holiday closures - The museums shut on several dates, so check before you book.
How to Beat the Crowds
The time you go matters, but how you book matters just as much. Three things make the biggest difference:
* Book a timed slot in advance - Even in low season the ticket queue at the door can run one to two hours. A pre-booked skip-the-line entry ticket lets you walk past it. Pick the earliest slot offered.
* Take an early morning tour - These get you in at or before opening, so you see the galleries and the Sistine Chapel at their calmest. It is the surest way to beat the crowds in peak season.
* Consider a Friday evening - In the warmer months the museums run special late openings on Friday evenings, which are quieter than the daytime. Our Vatican evening and night tour guide covers those.
Frequently Asked Questions
What is the best time of day to visit the Vatican Museums?
The first hour after the 8:00 am opening is the quietest, followed by the last two hours before the 8:00 pm close. The busiest time is the middle of the day, from about 10:00 am to 1:00 pm.
What is the least crowded day to visit the Vatican?
A weekday, ideally Tuesday to Thursday. Saturdays are the busiest open day, and the free last Sunday of the month is the single most crowded slot. Wednesday mornings can be slightly calmer because of the Papal Audience in the square. 
What is the best month to visit the Vatican Museums?
November, January, and February are the quietest, outside the holiday weeks. April through September is the busy season, with Easter and the summer months the most crowded.
Is the free last Sunday worth it?
Only if a free visit matters more than the crowds. Entry is free from 9:00 am to 2:00 pm, but it is the busiest day of the month, you cannot skip the line, and the queue is long. Most visitors are better off paying for a timed ticket on a quieter day.
Does the Wednesday Papal Audience affect the museums?
Yes, a little. The audience in St. Peter's Square draws some of the morning crowd away, so a Wednesday morning slot can be calmer. Once it ends, those visitors head to the museums, so go early.
Is summer a bad time to visit?
It is the busiest and hottest time, and the galleries can get stuffy, but it is still doable. Book the earliest slot you can, or a Friday evening opening, and bring water.
