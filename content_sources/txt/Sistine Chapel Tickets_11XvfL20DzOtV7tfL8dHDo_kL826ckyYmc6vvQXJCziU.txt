Sistine Chapel Tickets
Quick answer: There is no separate Sistine Chapel ticket. The chapel sits at the far end of the Vatican Museums, so every Vatican Museums ticket and tour includes it. That means your real choice is how you want to visit: a self-guided entry ticket from €33, a guided tour, an early morning tour before the crowds, or a private tour. All of them end at the Sistine Chapel with Michelangelo's ceiling and the Last Judgment. 


Looking for Sistine Chapel tickets is where most people get caught out, because the chapel cannot be booked on its own. 
It is the last and greatest room on the Vatican Museums route, so whichever Vatican and Sistine Chapel ticket you pick, you walk the galleries first and finish at Michelangelo's masterpiece.
Book ahead, because queues at the door can run two to three hours in peak season, and photography and talking are not allowed inside the chapel.

What you see in the Sistine Chapel
The Sistine Chapel is the reason most people come, and it rewards knowing what you are looking at before you arrive.
* Michelangelo's ceiling (1508 to 1512), with the nine scenes from Genesis down the centre, including the famous Creation of Adam where God and Adam reach towards each other.
* The Last Judgment (1535 to 1541), the vast fresco on the altar wall, painted by Michelangelo decades later, showing Christ judging humanity.
* The wall frescoes by earlier Renaissance masters such as Botticelli and Perugino, with scenes from the lives of Moses and Christ, often overlooked below Michelangelo's ceiling.
Because the chapel is the end of the route, you walk through the rest of the museums first. Our Gallery of Maps and Raphael Rooms guides cover the highlights on the way.
Rules inside the Sistine Chapel
The chapel is a consecrated space, and the rules are enforced by the guards.
* Silence - You are asked to stay quiet inside, and guards call for "Silenzio" when it gets loud.
* No photos or video - Photography and filming are not allowed at all in the Sistine Chapel, though they are fine in the general galleries without flash.
* Hats off, and keep moving so others can see.
* Dress code - Shoulders and knees must be covered, for men and women, across the whole museum. See the Vatican dress code guide.
Is there a separate Sistine Chapel Ticket?
No, and this is the thing to understand before you book. You cannot buy a ticket for the Sistine Chapel by itself. 
The chapel is the final room of the Vatican Museums, reached by walking the whole route past the Gallery of Maps and the Raphael Rooms, so access always comes bundled into a Vatican Museums and Sistine Chapel ticket.
That is good news, because it means every option includes the chapel. The decision is not which Sistine Chapel ticket to buy, but how you want to see the museums that lead to it: on your own, with a guide, early in the morning, or privately.
Opening Hours and Best Time to Visit
The Vatican Museums, and so the Sistine Chapel, are open Monday to Saturday from 8:00 am to 8:00 pm, with the last entry around 6:00 pm. They are closed on Sundays, except the last Sunday of the month when entry is free from 9:00 am to 2:00 pm, which is also the most crowded day. Hours vary by season, so check when you book.
The quietest times are the first slot of the day or the late afternoon, which is exactly why the early morning tour is popular. Our best time to visit and opening hours guides have the detail.
Frequently Asked Questions
Is there a separate ticket for the Sistine Chapel?
No. The Sistine Chapel is part of the Vatican Museums and sits at the end of the route, so it is included with every Vatican Museum ticket and tours. You cannot buy a chapel-only ticket.
Is the Sistine Chapel free?
Only on the last Sunday of the month, when the Vatican Museums offer free entry from 9:00 am to 2:00 pm. It is also the busiest day, so most visitors book a paid, timed ticket to skip the queue.
Can you take photos in the Sistine Chapel?
No. Photography and video are not allowed inside the Sistine Chapel. You can take photos without flash in the other galleries.
How do I skip the line for the Sistine Chapel?
Book a timed Vatican Museums and Sistine Chapel ticket or tour in advance. The queue to buy tickets at the door can run two to three hours in high season, which a pre-booked ticket lets you skip.
How long does it take to reach the Sistine Chapel?
Plan on about 2.5 to 3 hours to walk the museums and reach the chapel at a steady pace. Guided tours take a direct route and run 2 to 3.5 hours depending on the option.
