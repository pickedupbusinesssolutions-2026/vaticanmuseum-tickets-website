How to Choose the Right Tour
If the list feels like a lot, narrow it down with these four questions.
* What is your budget? The self-guided ticket is cheapest, a standard guided tour is the mid-range all-rounder, and private or breakfast tours are the premium picks.
* How much do you mind crowds? If busy galleries would spoil it, book an early morning or evening tour. If you do not mind, a daytime guided tour is fine.
* Are you in a group? For four or five people, a private tour often costs less per person than booking separate small-group spots, and you get a guide to yourselves.
* How much time do you have? With one packed day in Rome, a Vatican and Colosseum combo covers both icons. With more time, keep them separate and go at a calmer pace.
Whatever you choose, make sure it says skip-the-line or priority entry. That is the single feature that saves you the most time.
What to Watch Out For
A few honest warnings before you book. Skip-the-line is standard on any good tour, so you do not need to pay a premium for it as if it were special, just check it is included. 
Ignore the street sellers near the Vatican offering to "skip the line," and book with a reputable operator online instead, so you have a confirmed ticket and free cancellation. 
Remember that on Wednesday mornings St. Peter's Basilica is closed to tours because of the Papal Audience, so a tour that day spends the time in the galleries instead. And dress for entry: shoulders and knees must be covered, or you can be turned away.
Are Vatican Tours Worth It
For most visitors, yes. The Vatican Museums are huge and busy, and a tour saves you the queue and turns a confusing walk into a clear one with the highlights explained. 
The exception is if you are on a tight budget, have already read up on what you want to see, and do not mind navigating alone, in which case a self-guided ticket does the job for less. If you want help weighing the cost against the gate price, our Vatican ticket prices guide breaks it down. 
Frequently Asked Questions
What is the best Vatican tour?
For most first-time visitors, the best Vatican tour is a standard guided tour of the Vatican Museums, Sistine Chapel, and St. Peter's Basilica, at around €125. It skips the line, includes a guide, and uses the shortcut into the basilica. If you want the cheapest way in, a self-guided skip-the-line ticket from about €33 is the best value.
What is the best skip-the-line Vatican tour?
Any of the guided tours here include skip-the-line entry, so the best one is the standard guided tour for most people. For a self-guided option, the skip-the-line entry ticket gets you past the main queue without a guide.
What is the best time to take a Vatican tour?
Early morning is best for crowds, as you get in before the galleries fill up. Evening tours on select dates are also quiet. Avoid the middle of the day in summer, which is the busiest and hottest.
Do all Vatican tours include St. Peter's Basilica?
No. The guided, early morning, private, breakfast, and combo tours include a guided walk into St. Peter's Basilica. The self-guided entry ticket and most evening tours do not, so you would visit the basilica on your own. 
Is a private Vatican tour worth the money?
For a group of four or five, yes. A private tour is a flat price for up to five people, so it can cost less per person than separate small-group spots, and you get a guide to yourselves at your own pace. For one or two people, a shared guided tour is cheaper.
How far in advance should I book?
As early as you can. The official site and the best time slots sell out weeks ahead in peak season. Booking a week or more out gives you the pick of times, and most tours offer free cancellation if your plans change.
