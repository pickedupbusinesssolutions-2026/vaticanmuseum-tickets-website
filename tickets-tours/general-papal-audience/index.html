Papal Audience

A papal audience, or general audience, is a public gathering held on most Wednesday mornings when the Pope is in Rome, in St. Peter's Square. The Pope gives a short teaching, prayers are read in several languages, and it ends with the Apostolic Blessing. It is not a Mass. Tickets from the Vatican are always free, and you can request them through the Prefecture of the Papal Household, the US Visitors Office, or the Swiss Guard. The audience itself lasts about 1.5 to 2 hours. If you would rather not handle the tickets, the early queues, and finding a seat yourself, you can book a guided experience that takes care of all of it.

The Vatican gives out the tickets for free, so the first half of this guide is simply how to get them yourself.

The hard part is the crowd. Thousands of people come, the security lines start early, and seats are first come, first served.

What is a Papal Audience

A papal audience is a public gathering where the Pope, currently Pope Leo XIV, meets pilgrims and visitors from around the world.

It is a spiritual event, but it is important to know that it is not a Mass. Instead, it is built around a short teaching and a blessing from the Pope, and it is open to everyone, so you do not need to be Catholic to go.

At the end, the Holy Father leads the Our Father and gives the Apostolic Blessing to everyone present, and to their families and any religious items they have brought with them.

Before all that, the Pope rides through the square in the popemobile to greet the crowd, which is the moment most people hope to catch up close.

Papal Audience Schedule 2026

The general audience is held on most Wednesday mornings when the Pope is in Rome, but there are gaps in the calendar, so always check before you plan around one.

  • Wednesdays: The usual day, most weeks of the year.
  • Summer: Audiences are often suspended or moved indoors during the hottest weeks, in the month of August, and may be held in the air-conditioned Paul VI Audience Hall or paused while the Pope is away.
  • Feast days and travel: There is no general audience on major feast days, when a papal Mass may take place instead, or when the Pope is travelling.

Because the dates move, check the Vatican's official calendar for the exact Wednesdays before you book flights or a hotel around an audience. If your trip does not line up with a Wednesday, see the other ways to see the Pope below.

How to Get Free Papal Audience Tickets

This is the part to get right: papal audience tickets are always free, issued by the Vatican, and no one needs to pay for the ticket itself. If you prefer a guided visit, then it is a paid one.

There are three official ways to get one:

  • The Prefecture of the Papal Household - The Vatican runs an online booking system for the lay faithful to request free tickets for general audiences. This is the main official route.
  • The US Visitors Office (Pontifical North American College) - Visitors, especially those from the US, can request tickets by email through this office, which is often the easiest way to arrange pickup. Requests are best made at least two weeks ahead.
  • The Swiss Guard - If you have not booked in advance, you can sometimes request tickets in person the day before at the Sant'Anna gate, subject to availability.

Tickets are collected the afternoon before the audience, and they cannot be mailed or emailed. Request as early as you know your dates, because popular weeks fill up.

How Long Does the Papal Audience Last

The audience itself runs about 1.5 to 2 hours, from the Pope's arrival through the teaching and the final blessing.

Plan for the whole morning, though, because you need to arrive early to clear security and find a seat, and the crowd takes time to move in and out.

The guided experience takes about three and a half hours from the meeting point to the end.

What Happens During the Audience

The order of the event is the same each week. It opens with short readings on a theme, read in several languages, including Italian, English, French, German, Spanish, Portuguese, Polish, and Arabic. The Pope then gives a main address and a short teaching, followed by prayers.

At the close, the Holy Father leads the Our Father and gives the Apostolic Blessing. That blessing extends to everyone present, to their loved ones at home, and to any religious items they have brought, which is why many people carry rosaries or medals to be blessed.

There is also special seating for newlyweds, known as sposi novelli, who often attend in their wedding clothes.

Where It Takes Place

The audience is normally held outdoors in St. Peter's Square, which holds well over 50,000 people. You can read more about the space in our St. Peter's Square guide.

In extreme weather, heavy rain, or high summer heat, the Vatican may move the audience indoors to the Paul VI Audience Hall, which is much smaller at around 6,000 seats.

This is one more reason the guided experience helps, since the team knows on the day where to go.

Dress Code and Rules

The audience is a religious event, so the Vatican dress code applies. Shoulders and knees must be covered, for men and women, and sleeveless tops, shorts, and short skirts are not allowed. Our Vatican dress code guide has the full details.

A few more things to know:

  • Security is airport-style - Everyone passes through metal detectors, and the queues build early, so arriving ahead of time matters.
  • Seating is first come, first served - No operator can promise a specific seat. The earlier you are in the square, the closer you can get.
  • Getting there - The square is a short walk from the Ottaviano metro stop. See our how to get to the Vatican guide.

Insider Tips for the Best Experience

A few things that make a real difference on the day.

  • Aim for an aisle by the barriers - The Pope travels through the square in the popemobile before taking the stage. A spot next to the barriers along those aisles gives you the best chance of a close photo, a wave, or a blessing.
  • Bring items to be blessed. -The final blessing covers any rosaries, crucifixes, medals, or holy pictures you have with you, so bring anything you want blessed and hold it during the blessing.
  • Plan for the sun - The square has almost no shade, and Rome mornings can pass 30 to 35 degrees Celsius in summer. Bring a hat, sunscreen, and water. There are public drinking fountains, the nasoni, around the square, and small folding umbrellas are allowed for shade.

Other Ways to See the Pope

If a Wednesday does not fit your trip, there is another option that needs no ticket at all.

The Sunday Angelus takes place at 12:00 pm most Sundays, when the Pope appears at the window of the Apostolic Palace over St. Peter's Square.

He gives a short talk of around 15 minutes, leads the Angelus prayer, and gives a blessing. No booking is needed, just clear security in the square by about 11:15 am.

After the Audience

The audience ends around 11:00 to 11:30 am, and the timing lines up well with the rest of a Vatican day.

St. Peter's Basilica is closed to the public during the audience, but the doors reopen once it finishes. Because you are already inside the square and past security, you can walk straight up the steps into the Basilica without joining the long outside queue.

If you want the view over Rome, the dome climb ticket office is on the right of the Basilica portico, at about €8 for the stairs or €10 for the lift.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are papal audience tickets free?

Yes. The Vatican always issues general audience tickets free of charge. You can request them through the Prefecture of the Papal Household, the US Visitors Office, or the Swiss Guard. A paid guided experience is optional and covers the logistics, not the ticket itself.

How much does the guided papal audience experience cost?

The Vatican tickets are free, but the optional guided experience, which handles the tickets, security, and seating, is €38 for adults (15 and over), €33 for children (4 to 14), and free for infants under 4. Prices can change, so check the booking page.

What day is the papal audience?

Papal audience is held on most Wednesday mornings when the Pope is in Rome. There are exceptions for summer, feast days, and papal travel, so check the Vatican calendar before you plan around one.

How long is the papal audience on Wednesday?

The audience itself lasts about 1.5 to 2 hours. Allow the whole morning once you add early arrival, security, and getting in and out of the crowd.

Is the papal audience a Mass?

No. It is a public gathering with readings, a teaching from the Pope, prayers, and the Apostolic Blessing, but it is not a Catholic Mass.

Do I need a ticket for the papal audience?

Yes, a free ticket is needed for the Wednesday general audience. Whereas, the Sunday Angelus needs no ticket at all.

What should I wear to the papal audience?

Cover your shoulders and knees. No sleeveless tops, shorts, or short skirts, as the dress code is enforced at security.

Can I get close to the Pope?

A close view of the Pope is possible but not guaranteed. Getting to an aisle spot by the barriers early gives you the best chance as the Pope passes in the popemobile.
